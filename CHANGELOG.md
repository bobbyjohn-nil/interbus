# Changelog

All notable changes to the game are recorded here, newest first, in words a player would use.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/2.0.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

**Versions happen only when the owner says so.** Every change a player would notice adds its entry under [Unreleased] as it lands. A numbered version is created only when the owner types `/ship <version>`, which moves everything under [Unreleased] into a dated version, commits `Release v<version>`, merges it through a pull request and tags `v<version>`. Nothing else adds a version, bumps a version number or creates a `v*` tag: not the end of a session, not a milestone, not a plan. `node scripts/changelog.mjs check` runs in CI and fails any version not added by its own `Release v<version>` commit, and any later edit to released notes. It cannot tell who made that commit; the owner's yes inside `/ship` is the guard.

### Added

- The compass, in the corner above the map credits: its needle points north and leans as the map tilts. Click it to tilt the flat map into 3D, to turn a turned map back to north, or to lay a map that faces north flat again, as T does. Drag with the right mouse button (Ctrl-drag on a Mac, or two fingers) to tilt the map as far as you like between 25° and 60°; let go below 12° and it snaps flat. The buildings fade between flat and standing instead of jumping. In Low graphics the compass is off and says why.
- Double-click and hold on the map: hold still for half a second to switch between flat and 3D, or keep holding and drag up to tilt and down to flatten. A quick double-click still zooms in. With a tool out, a double-click never tilts, and the tool takes it as one click.
- Turn the map in 3D: drag sideways with the right button (or Ctrl-drag, or double-click and drag), or press Q and E; N or the compass faces north again. The flat map always faces north.
- The demand menu, a small chip beside the compass: show or hide where people live (purple) and where they work (blue), each on its own. It says how many people and jobs each stands for, and this browser remembers what you chose.
- Low graphics: on a computer that draws the map in software, the game keeps the map flat and its buses at a steady 30 frames a second or fewer, and says so when you press T. Without graphics acceleration at all, a card asks you to turn it on in the browser's settings, and your game, Saves and Export still work. If the browser takes the map's graphics away mid-game, a card says so with Reload. Add ?fps to the address to see the frame rate.
- Home: the game opens on a home screen over the paused map. Continue goes on with the game you were playing, named by its company, day and cash; New game starts a new company, asking first when it would replace that game (your slots stay); Saves opens your slots. Back to Home, in Saves, pauses and returns there.
- The save list: each slot in Saves names its buses beside its day, company, cash and lines, and when you saved it by your own clock. A slot can be deleted (Saves asks first), and a save set aside by a new build is listed there until you export it as a file or delete it.
- Express lines: a line of 5 km or more whose stops stand at least 1.2 km apart on average runs express. Riders feel its ride a little quicker and mind its fare half as much, so it draws more of them over the long trips it serves, though a line with more stops on the same street can still carry more. The line card says Express beside its name, and the new-line card says so as you draw one.
- Snow: now and then it snows for a day or two, named on the midnight card the evening before ("Snow tomorrow, for 1 day"). Every bus drives slower in the snow, so a round trip takes about a quarter longer (the line card says how much) and a line without a spare bus runs a little less often until it stops. Riders' own rides are no slower.
- Festivals: now and then a festival gathers near one of your busier stops for a day or two, named the evening before and ringed on the map. Trips to and from anywhere within about 900 m of it are 2.6 times as many while it lasts, enough to carry a RIDERSHIP contract through a day or two, though not for eight.
- Medals: bronze for a quarter's report card of 55 or better, silver for 25,000 trips carried, and gold for a quarter of 75 or better with no grade under 60 on a day of 5,000 trips. The midnight card says when you win one, the medals you hold show as dots on the Contracts chip, and the Contracts panel lists all three with the trips left to silver.
- Silver unlocks the Goliath: from the midnight that brings silver, the 115-seat bus is for sale on the line and draft cards.
- Edit route, on a line's panel: the line's stops listed in order and numbered on the map, like a trip planner. Drag a stop, or move it up or down, to reorder it; × takes it off; a click on the map adds a stop at the end, or adds the stop you click. The card says how much longer or shorter the route drives, and prices the new stops, any bus the longer round trip needs, and the stops it leaves alone, which come down for half their cost. Save route does it all at once, buses already on their way finish their trip as they set out, and Undo takes it back while paused.
- Build, in the dock: one button opens Place a stop, Draw a line and Place a depot.
- Place a stop on its own: a card at the pointer says whether a stop can go there and that it costs $4,000, or names the stop already standing within 250 m, which a line can use instead. The stop waits, ringed in black, until a line calls there: draw a line through it or add it in a line's Edit route, and the line uses it without buying another. Its card says no line stops there yet, and Take it down gives back $2,000.
- Roadworks: now and then the city digs up as much as 400 m of a street one of your lines drives, for 3 to 6 days. You hear the evening before, on the day card ("Roadworks on Huntington Avenue tomorrow, for 4 days"), and the street shows hatched on the map. While the works are on it shows solid, and every line through it drives that stretch at about two-thirds speed, so its round trip is longer; a line without the buses for the longer trip runs a little less often until they end.
- The line card shows the line's round trip at peak and off-peak, and how much roadworks on its streets add to it.
- RIDERSHIP contracts: beside the ones asking for a bus every so often, the city now offers contracts for 150, 400 or 800 paying riders a day boarding near a landmark, at $60,000, $150,000 or $240,000. Every stop within a walk of the landmark counts, all your lines together, but a rider changing onto your line there boards free and is not counted. The card shows today's boardings against the ask. Every six offers hold each of the six asks once.
- Rush hour slows your buses: on main streets a bus drives about 11% slower than the day's average at the peaks and 11% quicker off-peak, on motorways a little more and on side streets hardly at all. Each line's round trips, the buses it needs and the bus count on its cards follow, so a busy line on main streets may need one more bus at peak. Riders feel it too: across Inner Boston about 7% fewer ride at the peaks and 7% more off-peak, and the day's riders hardly change.
- Take a stop off a line from the stop's card: the line runs on without it, and a stop no other line calls at comes down, giving back half of what it and its fittings cost ($2,000 for a Sign). A line keeps at least two stops.
- Riders change buses: a trip can ride two of your lines, changing at a stop both call at or walking to the other line's stop up to 250 m away. A change costs riders that walk, a wait for the second bus and a few minutes' bother that a better stop eases (6 minutes at a Sign or Shelter, down to 2 at a Transfer Hub). Nobody changes onto a line they could have walked to from home, or off one that already takes them to work.
- A trip pays one fare, to the line it boards first, and the city's subsidy comes once a trip too, so the second bus is free. Splitting a line in two never earns more than running it whole.
- A stop's card says how many of the riders boarding there change from another line.
- Export session, in Saves: a file of this session from the quarter's start (or the last reload), with every command you gave, that replays to exactly the same game. Attach it to a bug report.
- Contracts: the city offers three at a time, each near a landmark such as Longwood or Copley Square, asking for a bus every 10, 15 or 20 minutes and paying $240,000, $150,000 or $60,000. Take one with a click; meet it at a stop within a walk, at peak and off-peak alike, for a quarter in a row (8 days) within two, and it pays as a Contracts row on the midnight card. Its card shows how often a bus comes there today against the ask.
- The checklist's last step, Take a contract, opens the contracts; once you take one the checklist is done and a Contracts chip takes its place.
- The preview opens on real Boston: streets, buildings, the Charles, parks and neighborhood names from Back Bay to Cambridge, drawn flat in the light theme and set in Afacad Flux. Zoom in to see individual buildings.
- The build number of the version you are looking at sits in the bottom-right corner, beside the map credits.
- Press T to tilt the map into 3D, with Boston's buildings standing up from its streets; press T again to lay it flat. Four buildings in five stand at their measured height, from OpenStreetMap or, where it has none, Overture Maps (credited in the corner); the rest stand about three stories.
- Everything beyond the play area is grayed out behind a dashed edge, so you can see where the board ends.
- Dots show where people live (purple) and where they work (blue) from Back Bay to Longwood, sized by how many.
- Point at a dot to see how many it stands for and how many more are within a walk: on a home dot, the people who live there and within a walk of it; on a job dot, the jobs there and within a walk.
- A new company starts with $474,548: enough for a depot, four stops and the two buses a line every 20 minutes needs, with some to spare.
- Found your company first: give it a name and pick its color on the founding card, where your starting cash shows.
- A checklist chip names the next thing to do: place a depot, draw a line, press play, close a day in profit, take a contract.
- Place a depot, from Build: the industrial land where one may stand turns green, and a card beside the pointer says whether this spot works and what it costs ($150,000 for the first).
- Draw a line, from Build: click the map to place stops (a click near a stop you already have joins it), each numbered in order. A card shows the stops, the distance, how many people live within a walk, the buses running every 20 minutes needs and the price; Create buys it all, once you have a depot.
- Riders pay their fares, the city adds its subsidy, and at midnight a card lists the day's money, adding up to the change in cash.
- Buses run: each line's Sparrows pull out of the depot before the first departure, drive the line at its headways with a short stop at each stop, and go home after the last trip. Pause, and they stop where they are.
- Each line has an inspector: click the line on the map or Lines in the dock to see its riders, its buses, its fare and its peak, off-peak and overnight headways, and Edit route. A headway that needs more buses says how many and what they cost before you buy them.
- The tools sit in a dock along the bottom of the screen, and the day, the time, the speed, cash and riders a day sit in a row of chips along the top, leaving the city the rest of the screen.
- When something can't be done, a notice in the bottom-left corner says why for a few seconds.
- Riders weigh the wait: up to a bus every 12 minutes most of them time their trip to the timetable, but a bus every hour carries few, so a line pays best every 10 to 15 minutes.
- Money can run out: a lender lets cash go down to -$150,000, charging 0.06% a day on what you owe as an Interest row, and turns away a purchase that would go further. A day that ends below the limit starts a red countdown chip ("5 days to get above -$150k"); five in a row and the lender calls in the loan, but Restart this quarter takes you back to the start of the quarter, or to the save you loaded during it.
- Every eight days the quarter closes with a report card on the midnight card: Coverage, Reliability, Comfort and Money, each out of 100. A good card earns a grant from the city and a poor one a fine, as one of the day's rows.
- Saves, at the top right: three slots to keep a game in beside the one that saves itself as you play, and Export and Import to keep a game as a file. A file the game cannot read is turned away with a note, and the game you are playing stays as it was.
- A save made on an earlier map of Boston carries on when the map is updated, its stops moved onto the new map's streets. If a stop has no street near it any more, the save is kept aside and a note says so.
- Undo while paused: the Undo chip (or Ctrl+Z, ⌘Z on a Mac) takes back the last thing you did since you paused, a depot, a line, a headway, a bus model, a fare or a stop, cash and all. Once the clock has run, it says so instead.
- Finance, in the dock, in three views. Overview shows your cash, yesterday's and the quarter's running money, and a chart of the last 16 days (from Day 0 early in a game): cash as a line, and below it each day's money in and running costs as bars, with a mark on a day you bought or sold something; point at a day for its amounts. Breakdown lists every kind of money in and out for today, yesterday and this quarter, beside what the day is forecast to bring, adding up to the change in cash; each group's heading gives its totals and folds it, and Buying and building opens with a click. Lines lists each line's riders, fare and money, and opens a line to show what it earns and spends in a day.
- Make a stop nicer from its card: a Shelter ($12,000), a Station ($35,000), an Interchange ($90,000, with 3 lines calling) or a Transfer Hub ($200,000, with 5). A nicer stop feels closer on foot, so more people walk to it; stepping it back down returns half of what the step cost.
- Click a stop to see its day: how many board and get off there, and what it earns. A stop with fewer than 5 boardings a day reads Quiet, and one losing more than $20 a day reads Losing money (Loss on the map), with a ring around it on the map.
- Set each line's fare, from $1.00 to $5.00, in Finance under Lines. Riders on short hops mind a dear fare more than riders going far, the city's subsidy shrinks as the fare climbs past $2.25 and is gone at $5.00, and Finance names the fares that make the line the most money.
- Buses fill up: when more riders want a line than its seats carry, the extra riders are turned away in that half-hour, and the line's card says when it is full ("Full at 17:00") and how many riders a day it turns away. Running more often or with bigger buses carries them.
- Two more buses: the Metro 40 (70 seats, $260,000) and the Goliath (115 seats, locked until a silver medal). Each line runs one model: pick it on the new-line card, or swap a line's buses on its card, where the old ones sell for half their price. Bigger buses are drawn longer on the map.
- The game saves itself in this browser as you play, so a reload carries on where you left off. A save from an older build is kept aside, a new game starts, and a note says so.
- Boston starts with no buses, trains or ferries of its own: the map carries none of today's stops, stations, rail lines or ferry routes, and the bus-only streets show as ordinary streets your buses can use.
- Running a line costs money: $0.90 for each kilometer a bus drives, $26 an hour for each driver on the road, the drive from the depot and back, and $300 a day for each depot. The midnight card lists each, and a Costs/day chip shows what the day is forecast to cost.
- Stops cost upkeep: a thousandth of what a stop cost to build, every day, so a Sign costs $4 a day, a Shelter $16, a Station $51, an Interchange $141 and a Transfer Hub $341. A stop's card says its upkeep and the next tier's, its upkeep counts in what the stop earns, and Costs/day includes it.

### Changed

- A line drawn as a game starts, as a new game's first line usually is, skips the departures no bus can reach from the depot in time, such as its 05:00 departure: its buses pull out of the depot first, as they do for a line drawn later in the day.
- Undo names taking a stop off a line as "Undo: Take Stop 2 off Line 1", shorter than before, so it fits beside the other chips.
- Before you have founded a company there is no game to lose, so Load in Saves opens the slot's game straight away.
- Clicking the same button several times in quick succession counts as one press, so a double-click never does a thing twice. To take back several things with the Undo chip, pause between clicks, or press Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) each time.
- Saves lists the autosave as it lists a slot, with its day, company, cash, lines and buses, and when it was saved, in place of the line that said the game saves itself.
- Save here over a slot that already holds a save asks first, so it is never replaced by accident.
- Time runs in minutes and days on a calendar of 8-day quarters, four to a year. The top bar names the year and quarter, then the day of the quarter and the time ("Year 1, Q2 Day 4, 08:10"), and the day card, Home, Saves and the medals say the date the same way.
- Speeds are true multiples of real time: 1× runs a game second a second, then 60×, 600×, 2,700× and 5,400×, picked from a speed menu beside Play and Pause. Space pauses and plays again, and the keys 1 to 5 pick a speed. At 1× and 60× the buses glide instead of stopping and jumping.
- Contracts ask for a quarter in a row, 8 days, within two quarters, 16 days, and an offer nobody takes is withdrawn after a quarter.
- The quarter's report card pays $1,280 a point over 55 and fines $6,400 below 35: the same a day as before, over the shorter quarter.
- The game saves at least every 30 seconds while time runs, as well as every game hour.
- The count of people within a walk shows only while the pointer is on a dot, instead of wherever it is.
- Finance says where the money goes, in plain words, under Money in, Rewards, Running costs and Buying and building: buses bought and sold, new stops, stop upgrades and stops sold back, and depots each have a row of their own instead of one Purchases row; "Bus fuel and maintenance" is the cost of every kilometer driven, "To and from the depot" replaces Deadhead, and each row's tooltip says what it covers. The midnight card uses the same names.
- Finance opens on an overview with a chart of the last 16 days (from Day 0 early in a game), the table of every row is under Breakdown with Buying and building folded, and each line's money is under Lines.
- Place a depot and Draw a line moved into Build, in the dock, beside Place a stop.
- Saves moved out of the dock to a button at the top right.
- Panels stay open together and move: Lines, Finance, Saves, Contracts and a stop's card can all be open at once, each opening in a free place beside the others. Drag one by its heading anywhere on the screen and it opens there again for the rest of the session; press one to bring it to the front. A tool's card takes its own place too, so a tool stays out while panels open, and Escape puts everything away. Edit route stands in for the line's panel while it is open.
- The bus and headway menus on a line's panel and on the new-line card are the game's own, like the speed and fare menus; a locked bus is grayed out and says a silver medal unlocks it.
- Extending a line from either end and inserting a stop, on a line's panel, are now Edit route, which also reorders the line and takes stops off it.
- A line's fare and its day forecast moved from its card to Finance, under Lines. The line card says the fare and links there, and otherwise shows only the line.
- Your starting cash is on Day 0, before the first day, so day 1's card and Finance's columns show only what day 1 earned and spent.
- Finance forecasts a day's loan interest while your cash is below zero.
- Words across the game start with a capital and read as whole sentences: the headway menus say Off and Every 10 min, the new-line card says "Running every 20 min needs 2 Sparrows", the founding card names its colors (Red, Blue and so on) instead of their codes, a stop's flags read Quiet and Losing money instead of QUIET and LOSS, the fare hint reads "Most profit at", and the game's name, the build chip and a few notices are capitalized and reworded.
- The dock's buttons are a little slimmer, to leave room on screen for the compass and the demand menu.
- Changing a line while its buses run no longer throws them across the map: a bus already on its way finishes its trip on the route and the times it set out with, and the next departures follow the change as buses come free. That holds for taking a stop off, extending a line, a new headway or bus model, a new depot, and roadworks starting or ending.
- Riders a day, in the chips along the top, counts each trip once, even when it rides two lines.
- Where several of your lines serve the same trip, riders take whichever comes first: lines sharing a stretch split its riders by how often each runs, and together, coming more often, they carry more than any one would.
- The preview now plays all of Inner Boston, not just Back Bay to Longwood: Boston, Cambridge, Somerville and Brookline, with homes and jobs across all four and contracts offered at Kendall Square, Harvard Square and Downtown Crossing too. Demand is scaled so a first line from Copley to Longwood carries about what it did.
- How far people commute now follows Boston's own census records rather than a generic guess, so trips are a little shorter on average and a line from Copley to Longwood carries about 3% fewer riders.

### Fixed

- The cards for a browser without graphics acceleration, and for one that takes the map's graphics away mid-game, say your game is saved only while it is saving itself. While it isn't (the Saves chip says "Autosave off") or the browser keeps no saves at all, they say so and offer Export this game, which on the lost-graphics card comes before Reload. Before, they said your game was saved, and the lost-graphics card's Reload then lost everything since the last save the browser had kept: the whole company when it kept nothing, or all the play since it ran out of room.
- With the game open in two tabs, the game saved last wins. Home names and continues the game saved last in either tab, when this build can play it, not the one the tab found when it opened. A tab left behind by the other tab's saves writes nothing over them: its Saves chip says "Autosave off", a note says another tab changed the autosave, and its Home names and continues the other tab's game. Before, the second tab's Continue played the older game and saved it over the other tab's newer days, fares and cash, without a word.
- The card that says the browser took the map's graphics away moves to the foot of the map while the midnight card or the lender's card is up, so you can read it and press its Reload, and their own buttons stay clear of it. Before, the lender's card, which can't be closed, hid all of it but the edge of Reload, and a midnight card hid its words.
- A contract asking for a bus every so often shows today's figure to the second within a minute of its ask, so a figure in red never reads as the ask itself. A line slowed by snow or roadworks, alone or beside another line at the stop, can come a second or so short: the card now says "Today: every 10 min 1 s" against every 10 min. Before, it said "every 10 min" (or "every 20.0 min" against every 20 min) in red, so a missed day showed nothing of by how much. A figure that meets its ask within a minute reads to the second too, such as "every 9 min 30 s".
- Riders changing onto one of two lines that take them the rest of the way equally well, such as two lines with the same stops running as often, now split evenly between them however you drew them. Before, every rider changing onto the pair rode the line drawn last, so its riders a day read more than its twin's by all of them.
- Holding Enter or Space down on a button or in a menu presses it once, however long you hold it: a held Enter on a stop's Make it a Shelter no longer buys a Shelter and then a Station, on the Undo chip no longer takes back several things, on Play no longer flips time between playing and paused, and on Export this game no longer downloads a file again and again. In the demand menu it turns Homes or Jobs on or off once, and in a dropdown, such as the speed menu, it picks once and leaves the list closed.
- When Escape closes a menu you opened with the mouse, such as the speed menu or the demand menu, Space goes back to pausing and playing instead of opening it again. One you opened from the keyboard still opens again with Space.
- In Saves, a question you open from the keyboard (Delete, on a slot or a save set aside; Save here on a slot that holds a save; Load; or Import a save file) moves the focus to Keep it or Keep playing, so Space answers the question instead of playing or pausing the game behind Saves. Answering it from the keyboard in the game puts the focus back on the button that opened it, on its row once you delete the save in it, or on the Saves panel once you delete a save set aside, whose row goes with it.
- Pressing Tab into a panel you dragged partly past the right edge of the window no longer shifts the whole game left, with a gray band down the right side and panels opening out of place and jumping away from the pointer as you drag them. Instead, a panel you dragged partly past either edge moves far enough to show the button the keyboard reaches in it, and stays where it moved.
- When Boston's map can't be loaded, after a server error, a dropped connection or a map that never finishes loading, a card high on the screen says so, with Reload, above Home and the founding card. Before, the only word of it was hidden under Home, then under the founding card, with no Reload, and it blamed your computer. The card for a map that loads but draws nothing moved up too, with Reload. When Boston itself can't be loaded either, its own card says so alone.
- The founding card has Back to Home, so after New game you can go back to import a save file or load a slot instead. Before, nothing on the card left it.
- A game saved before its company was founded, loaded from a slot or a file, opens its founding card empty, on Red, as a new game does. Before, it showed the name and color of the company it replaced, and one press founded the loaded game under them.
- Holding down 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 picks that speed once, and time runs at it while you hold the key. Before, time all but stopped for as long as you held it, and the game saved itself again at every repeat of the key.
- When the game stops working while time runs, the map under "The game stopped working" rests, as it does while paused, with its buses where the game last had them, instead of keeping your computer busy drawing it again and again until you reload.
- Building and editing no longer slow down as your company adds depots: with ten lines and five depots on Inner Boston, the game's work after each change took about a fifth of a second and now takes about a tenth, as it did before the depots.
- Edit route names the room your depots really have: with every depot full, a route that needs more buses says "Your depots are full", as the headway card does, where it said "Depots have room for 2 more" beside "2 more Sparrows". With some room, it names that room, not that room plus the line's own buses.
- The quarter's report card counts the last day's loan interest in its Money grade, as it counts the other seven; before, that day's interest counted in no quarter. That interest is now charged on what you owe before the card's grant or fine comes in.
- Changing a line a second time in a day, after its buses have all switched over to the first change, no longer throws off that day's costs. Finance's Day forecast, in the Breakdown and under Lines, and Costs/day show the Bus fuel and maintenance, Driver wages and To and from the depot that night's midnight card banks. Before, they costed the hours before the first change as if it had been in place since midnight, so they could read well off what the day banked; and when the two changes came within the same hour, that hour was charged by the first change's timetable instead of what the buses did.
- When your browser has no room left for your game's own saves (its autosave, or the save of the quarter's start that Restart this quarter goes back to), the game says so: the notice reads "No room for saves: delete a save", and Saves says "No room for saves. Delete a save, or export this game to keep it." until each of them is kept. It used to say your browser wasn't keeping saves, which it still says when the browser blocks them.
- A Save here that doesn't fit says so on its slot's row in Saves, "Didn't fit: delete a save to make room", until you save into that slot or delete a save, or load, import or start a game, or Restart this quarter; where your browser blocks saves, the row says "Not kept: this browser isn't keeping saves". Before, it said your browser wasn't keeping saves, once each time the page loaded, and in Saves only until the game next saved itself, within a second at 5,400×, so a slot pressed again stayed Empty without a word.
- While the game isn't saving itself, because your browser has no room for its autosave or blocks saves, or an older save it couldn't set aside is in the way, the Saves chip at the top right says "Autosave off", in red while Saves is closed, until it saves again. Before, nothing on screen said so once the notice had gone. When the chips along the top fill the row, the Undo chip is cut a little shorter to make room for it.
- Changing a line again while some of its buses are still finishing a trip from an earlier change no longer makes them jump. If that change gave the line a new first stop (reordering its stops in Edit route so another comes first, adding a stop before the first or taking off its first stop) or swapped its buses for another model, those buses still drive back to the depot after their trip, instead of jumping to the line's new first stop or, once sold, running the new model's trips.
- A line you draw partway through the day starts the way each morning's service does: each bus pulls out of the depot in time for the first departure it can reach, and its drive shows under To and from the depot. So does overnight service turned on while all of a line's buses are back at the depot. Before, the buses appeared at the ends of the line or partway along it, and the hour you drew the line or turned overnight service on was charged as if they had run all of it.
- On the day you draw a line, Finance's Day forecast, in the Breakdown and under Lines, counts the line's fares and subsidy from the hour you drew it, and its Bus fuel and maintenance, Driver wages and To and from the depot from when its buses pull out, as that night's midnight card banks them, even after you change the line again that day. It used to count a whole day of each. Costs/day counts the line the same way; from the next day both count a whole day.
- Roadworks or snow starting or ending at midnight, after you changed a line earlier that day, no longer make the buses still out on your lines jump hundreds of meters along the street: they finish their trips as they began.
- A company you play for a long time keeps saving. Now days older than the last 16, which Finance's chart shows, are kept as totals, so a save stays about the same size however long you play. The autosave shrinks at its next midnight, the save from the quarter's start at the next quarter, and a slot when you next save into it. Your cash and every figure Finance, the day card and the report card show stay exactly as they were.

The Undo chip shows the whole of what it takes back wherever the row has room and ends in "…" where it hasn't; once it is as short as it goes, the Contracts chip, its medals kept whole, or the checklist's next step in its place, ends in "…" too. From the second quarter its axis carries the quarter (Q1 Day 1, Q2 Day 1) where it counted days since you founded the company (Day 1, Day 9), and pointing at a bar or a dot reads Year 1, Q2, Day 1. Your starting cash stays on Day 0, before the first quarter, and a label that would run into another is left out.

Home's card keeps its width: Start over's question and a long company name wrap instead of widening it. - When your browser has no room left for saves and you delete a save in Saves to make room, the game now saves at once what the browser refused to keep: your game, the save of the quarter's start that Restart this quarter goes back to, or both. While the page stays open, Saves keeps saying there is no room to save this game until each of them is kept. Before, the delete took that line down though nothing had been saved, and so did any other save the browser kept: when it refused the quarter's start and then kept your game's next save, the line went, though the quarter's start was never kept. A reload before the game next saved itself (your next command or pause, or within 30 seconds while time runs) lost what came since its last kept save. A quarter's start the browser had refused was never saved again, so after a reload Restart this quarter went back to an older save, or said there was none from this quarter's start.

Space or Enter moves it again, one step for each press even if you hold the key, instead of pausing or playing the game behind the editor, or doing nothing.

On Home a notice says "Not autosaving: see Saves" until you open Saves, and in the game the Saves chip says "Autosave off"; in Saves, the autosave's row names the old save and says this game isn't saving itself, with Export and Delete. Your new game saves itself again once you delete the old save.

Saves keeps one question open at a time, and a slot asking whether to load or delete its save no longer shows Save here beside the question.

- A new game, or a game loaded from Saves or Restart this quarter, starts clean: the old game's line tool, route editor, draft, day card, stop card, Lines panel and load notice ("Slot loaded", "Save file loaded", "Back to the start of the quarter") no longer come with it, so pointing at a dot shows its card again, no card shows a stop or line you never clicked in this game, and the new-line card starts on the Sparrow instead of a Goliath that game hasn't unlocked.
